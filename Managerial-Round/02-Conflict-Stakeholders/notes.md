# Conflict & Stakeholder Management

## Questions
- Tell me about a disagreement with your manager.
- Tell me about a difficult stakeholder.
- How did you influence someone without authority?
- When did you challenge a KPI definition?
- How do you say no to an unrealistic request?
- How do you explain technical limitations to business users?

## Real story candidate: product penetration metric (FMG)
Stakeholder asked for average products per customer. Product IDs represented roughly 1,400 SKUs while the business also had higher-level product categories. A raw SKU-based percentage appeared misleading. You clarified the business intent and suggested a category-level penetration definition that responds to the selected time period.

**Answer structure:** Initial ask → why the first definition was misleading → questions asked → alternative proposed → agreement/validation → decision supported. **Verify** final metric denominator (available vs active categories), adoption, and impact before claiming them.

## Real story candidate: global regional lead definitions (Dell)
Regional teams did not all define a lead identically. Explain how you identified differing regional keys, involved stakeholders, reconciled definitions and retested edge cases. For a failure question, own the initial incorrect assumption rather than blaming regional teams.

## Phrases
- “Before implementing it, I wanted to align on what the metric should represent.”
- “I presented both approaches and explained the trade-off.”
- “We agreed on a definition that better reflected the business question.”

## Practice drill
Answer: “A stakeholder insists on a metric you believe is misleading. What do you do?” Use Clarify → Evidence → Options → Agreement.
