# Mock Interviews & Improved Answers

## Goal
Sound like a senior BI professional: concise, specific, accountable and business-oriented.

## Daily 30–60 minute routine
- 5 min: Review one story and pick the question.
- 5 min: Write three answer anchors, not a script.
- 10–20 min: Answer aloud; record or transcribe.
- 10 min: Identify fillers, restarts, missing decisions and unclear outcome.
- 5–15 min: Retry with tighter structure and one follow-up question.

## Response frameworks
**Behavioural:** Situation → Task → Action → Result → Learning.
**Stakeholder:** Business question → Evidence → Trade-off → Recommendation.
**Executive update:** Observation → Business impact → Next action.
**Short response:** Think 5 seconds → speak 30–60 seconds → close with one sentence.

## Evaluation rubric (score 1–5)
1. Directly answers question.
2. Clear sequence and concise sentences.
3. Specific personal ownership.
4. Business outcome with credible evidence.
5. Calm delivery, minimal fillers and no unnecessary repetition.

## Practice queue
1. Tell me about yourself (60–90 sec).
2. Biggest accomplishment (Dell).
3. A mistake you made (lead definition).
4. Influencing a stakeholder (product penetration).
5. Initiative beyond role (Google Sheets automation).
6. End-to-end ownership (Dell / FMG).
7. Pressure and priorities (needs real incident).
8. Conflict with manager (needs real incident).
9. Critical feedback (needs real incident).
10. Changing requirements (use verified example).

## Sample: mistake question — talking points
“In a global marketing reporting project, I initially standardized the lead key across all regions. During validation, I discovered that some regions defined leads at a different grain. I flagged it, aligned the definitions with stakeholders, corrected the logic and retested the edge cases. The key lesson was to confirm metric grain by region before consolidating data.”
