# Question 1 — Conflicting stakeholder requirements

**Question:** Tell me about a time when two stakeholders had conflicting requirements. How did you resolve the disagreement while keeping the project on track?

**Practice assessment:** 6.5/10 (initial spoken response)

## Feedback
- Strengths: Proposed checking source data and requirements before choosing a definition; recognized that two valid business perspectives can justify separate metrics.
- Improve: Structure the answer using STAR; say *metric* rather than *matrix*; distinguish revenue from profit; avoid attributing disagreement to ego; close with documented definitions, approval, and an outcome.
- Technical distinction: Revenue minus expenses equals profit, not an alternative revenue definition. Gross sales, net sales, and recognized revenue may legitimately differ, depending on documented accounting rules.

## Corrected version of the user's approach (hypothetical response)
If Sales and Finance disagreed about a KPI, I would first understand the business purpose of each calculation. I would review their requirements, trace the figures to source data, and compare filters, transaction dates, and calculation logic. If both definitions were valid but measured different business concepts, I would recommend two clearly labeled metrics with agreed definitions. If they were intended to measure the same KPI, I would facilitate a discussion to agree on one standard definition. I would document the decision, obtain stakeholder sign-off, and validate the dashboard before release.

## Ideal model answer (illustrative, not a verified past achievement)
**Situation:** During a reporting project, imagine that Sales and Finance disagreed over the revenue figure displayed in a Power BI dashboard. Their reports applied different calculation rules, which put the release at risk.

**Task:** As the senior analyst, my responsibility would be to establish trustworthy definitions, align the stakeholders, and keep delivery moving.

**Action:** I would meet each team to clarify the business question behind its metric. Next, I would reconcile sample transactions and compare source systems, date logic, exclusions, adjustments, and calculation rules. I would present the differences and their business impact in a joint meeting. If the teams genuinely needed distinct measures, such as gross sales and net revenue, I would label and document both. If both reports purported to show the same KPI, I would seek agreement on one governed definition, with a named business owner. Finally, I would obtain sign-off, update the semantic model and metric dictionary, and communicate any schedule impact.

**Result:** The intended outcome would be agreed, auditable reporting definitions and a release plan stakeholders trust. In a real interview, replace this with the actual outcome and any verified measures of impact.

## Key takeaways
- One concept with two conflicting numbers: reconcile and standardize the definition.
- Two different valid business concepts: show two separately named metrics, not one ambiguously labeled metric with two values.
- Avoid invented achievements; personalize the STAR story using a real project and documented result.

## Follow-up: What if both stakeholders still disagree?

**Follow-up assessment:** 7/10. Strong evidence-based instinct: compare how the two calculations influence business decisions. Improvement: the largest apparent impact is not necessarily the most appropriate metric; decide based on the business question and governance rules.

**Corrected answer:** If the stakeholders still disagree, I would compare both calculations side by side and explain their impact on business decisions. I would clarify the purpose of each KPI and determine whether the definitions represent different concepts. If both are valid, I would recommend clearly labeling them separately. If the business requires one enterprise-wide definition and agreement remains impossible, I would present the evidence, trade-offs, and recommendation to the accountable metric owner for a final decision. I would document the decision and obtain sign-off before publishing.

**Model answer:** I would avoid choosing a KPI solely because it produces a more favorable result. I would build a reconciliation showing the calculation rules, data sources, and decision implications. I would facilitate a final alignment meeting, recommend separate measures where business purposes genuinely differ, and escalate any unresolved enterprise definition to the designated decision-maker. Once approved, I would update the metric dictionary and dashboard, confirm ownership, and communicate the outcome to both teams.
